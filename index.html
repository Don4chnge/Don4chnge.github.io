Valorian Intelligence

A smoke alarm for machine-learning models. It tells you when a model that is already in use has quietly stopped working.

01Why I built it

Why I built it

Most machine-learning projects stop once the model is trained and scores well on a test set. That is the easy half. In the real world a model that scored well in January can be quietly wrong by August, because the world it learned from has moved on. Nothing in the system announces it, and people keep trusting its decisions.

Banks, insurers and retailers make credit, fraud and stock decisions with models like these every day. I wanted a tool that watches a live model and raises its hand when it stops being trustworthy, without crying wolf every week.

The hardest case is a model that has broken while every input still looks perfectly normal. That is the case this project exists for.

02In plain English

What it does, in plain English

  • It asks two questions about every new batch of data. Has the information going into the model changed? And has the model itself stopped getting the right answers?
  • It gives one health score, the DriftScore. 100 means the model behaves as it did when it was trained; 0 means it has fallen apart. Managers get one number; engineers can drill into the detail behind it.
  • It avoids false alarms. A monitor that fires every week gets ignored. Valorian measures how big a change is, not only whether it exists, so small harmless shifts stay quiet.
  • It keeps a history, so drift becomes a trend you can see on a chart rather than a one-off number.

03Try it yourself

Try it yourself

DriftScore by batch · click a bar

  1. Open the live dashboard. It loads a credit-default model monitored across six monthly batches.

  3. Look at Drift over time. The shaded bands are the warning thresholds. A spike means the inputs changed; it does not by itself mean the model broke.

05Technical
implementation

How it works

  • Effect size before p-values. At 3,000 to 40,000 rows a KS test rejects almost anything, so Valorian leads with the Population Stability Index (stable < 0.10, investigate 0.10–0.25, significant > 0.25) and reports KS and chi-square p-values as supporting evidence.
  • Bin edges frozen from the training sample, so drift cannot partly cancel itself out by re-binning both samples together. Outer bins open to ±∞ so out-of-range production values are counted, not dropped.
  • Concept drift is tracked as rolling ROC-AUC against a frozen baseline. When performance drops with no input drift, the alert says so explicitly: suspect concept drift or a labelling fault.
  • Multivariate drift via a domain classifier: label reference rows 0 and current rows 1, train a depth-limited random forest, score by cross-validated ROC-AUC. Near 0.50 means no difference; its feature importances say which columns moved.
  • DriftScore weights performance 0.70, worst-feature PSI 0.20 and share of drifted features 0.10, with a documented reason for each weight. Without labels it falls back to an inputs-only mode and says so.
  • Every run is stored in SQLite and drawn by the Streamlit dashboard. GitHub Actions runs the 78-test suite on Python 3.10, 3.11 and 3.12.
  • A data-quality catch: the 2022 Q1 QLFS release stores employment status as text, with 1.797e+308 as a missing-value marker, which silently made that quarter show 0% employment. The loader fixes it.

01Why we built it

Why we built it

SABRIC reports R2.4 billion lost to digital banking crime in South Africa in 2025, and a GASA 2026 survey found only 6% of scam victims reported it to the authorities. Fake job offers, fake couriers, marketplace sellers, romance and crypto schemes: most victims never get a case that goes anywhere, for three reasons:

  • Evidence is messy. It sits in WhatsApp chats, screenshots and proof-of-payment slips that police and banks can’t easily use, and that can be questioned later.
  • Every report looks small on its own. One R3 500 loss gets ignored. Seven victims of the same syndicate is a case, but nobody connects them.
  • Scammers switch numbers. Blocking one number does nothing when they use a new one the next day.

02In plain English

What it does, in plain English

  1. Lock the evidence. Every chat, screenshot and payment slip gets a digital fingerprint the moment it’s reported. If even one pixel changes later, the fingerprint no longer matches.

  2. Pull out the scammer’s details. Bank accounts, phone numbers, links and rand amounts are found automatically, along with a timeline: offer, payment request, pressure, payment, silence.

  3. Link the victims. A "Scam Fingerprint" connects reports through shared accounts, links or the wording of the scam script, so a scammer who changes phone number is still linked.

  4. Write it up. It drafts a statement in the victim’s language, a bank fraud report, and a downloadable PDF case pack.

04Architecture

Architecture

How the parts fit together. Select any box to see what it does in plain words.

05Technical
implementation

How it works

  • Evidence locking: real SHA-256 of every file and of the chat text using the Web Crypto API, plus a case-manifest hash, so any later edit, missing file or swapped file is detectable.
  • Extraction engine: parses Android and iPhone WhatsApp exports and pulls South African bank accounts, +27 and 0XX phone numbers, links, emails and rand amounts, with scam keywords in five languages.
  • Privacy-preserving Scam Fingerprint: identifiers are stored only as keyed HMAC-SHA256 hashes, plus 3-word shingles of the message script, matched against a fictional network of 80 earlier reports and 3 syndicates.
  • Ubufakazi 2.0 backend (my part): a Telegram reporting bot with a quick-exit at every step and a FastAPI server on Render with Neon Postgres, server-side HMAC keys and RFC 3161 trusted timestamps.
  • Investigator console: password plus two-factor sign-in, CSRF protection, station isolation (officers see only their own station’s reports) and a hash-chained audit log that detects tampering.
  • Security by default: strict Content Security Policy, no third-party scripts or trackers, and all user text rendered with textContent so a malicious chat can’t inject code.
  • Follows forensic practice: the four NIST SP 800-86 stages (collection, examination, analysis, reporting), from a victim's phone to a linked syndicate case.
  • For banks: signed alerts on mule accounts with per-bank HMAC keys and a 5-minute replay window, plus a check before a customer pays a new beneficiary. One report alone never blocks an account, and banks see risk levels and counts, never names or chats.
  • Hardened: Argon2id passwords and account lockout, prompt-injection scanning on AI readings (which stay "unverified" until checked), OWASP Top 10:2025 mapping with live self-checks, and POPIA-minded data handling.
  • Tested: a 70-case, five-language test pack checked automatically in a headless browser; every case extracts the correct account, amount and scam type.

06Results and
evidence

Results and evidence

From the HackSecure 2026 pitch

  • Honest limits: the browser MVP keeps its HMAC key in the browser and saves added reports only on that device; the 2.0 backend moves keys to the server. Translations should be checked by fluent speakers. Nothing is connected to SAPS or a real bank, and no case numbers are issued.

Hung Council Watch

Which South African councils could lose their majority on 4 November 2026, and need a coalition to govern?

01Why we built it

Why we built it

South African local politics has fragmented sharply. In 2000, 70% of municipalities were dominated by one party. By 2021 it was 16%. After the 2016 election 27 councils were hung, meaning no party won a majority. After 2021 there were 72.

A hung council means coalition government, which has been linked to unstable administrations, frequent changes of mayor and disrupted service delivery. Voters, journalists, parties and the IEC all benefit from knowing where that is likely to happen next, before it does.

Our first hypothesis failed. Turnout does not follow an inverted U against competition. We published that result and used it to ask a better question.

02In plain English

What it does, in plain English

  • It learns from 20 years of results. For every municipality it looks at how big the leading party’s share was, how many parties really competed, and how that changed from 2000 to 2021.
  • It gives each council a risk score: the chance that no party wins a majority in 2026, from safe to likely hung.
  • It lets you test scenarios. What if leading parties lose another few points everywhere? What if one party’s supporters stay home on election day?
  • It runs in your browser. The trained model is built into the public dashboard, so anyone can explore it without installing anything.

05Technical
implementation

How it works

  • Data pipeline: cleaned each election in its own notebook, then linked 263 councils across the 2006, 2011 and 2016 boundary changes by code and name.
  • Two source-data errors found and fixed: the 2016 turnout column counted only the leading party’s votes, and the 2021 file had lost most of the Western Cape to Excel’s row limit. The 2021 data is now rebuilt in code from the nine provincial files, with a check that all 213 municipalities are present.
  • Features: Effective Number of Parties (Laakso–Taagepera), the leading party’s share, the winning margin and turnout.
  • Testing the first idea properly: Lind–Mehlum U-tests and within-municipality fixed-effects models showed no inverted-U relationship between turnout and competition, so the project moved to forecasting.
  • Model selection: KMeans competition regimes, then four models (a baseline, two logistic regressions and tuned XGBoost) compared with grouped and time-ordered validation. XGBoost won ordinary cross-validation but lost on future elections, so the simpler logistic model was chosen.
  • Deployment: a single-file D3.js dashboard on Netlify with a risk map, swing scenarios and an election-day simulator that runs the trained model in the browser. A validation layer checks the modelling panel and the dashboard data before every deploy.
  • Reproducible: relative paths throughout; the whole pipeline rebuilds from the raw IEC files in under two minutes.

06Results and
evidence

Results and evidence

  • Limits: the forecast uses past results only, with no polling, new parties or local events, which is why the dashboard offers scenarios. 2024 national results are the main planned addition. "Hung" is defined as the leading party winning under 50% of the PR vote, so exact seat rounding can occasionally differ.

Load Shedding’s Ripple Effect

Does the severity of Eskom’s load shedding measurably move South African retail sales, and by how much?

01Why I built it

Why I built it

Every South African felt load shedding, and everyone agrees it hurt the economy. Far fewer people can say by how much. I wanted to move from "it was bad" to a number someone could defend, using official data rather than a pre-cleaned dataset from the internet.

So I went to the source: Eskom’s own record of how many megawatts the national control centre ordered off the grid, and Stats SA’s monthly retail trade sales.

02In plain English

What it found, in plain English

  • When power cuts were heavier, shops sold less compared with the same month a year before. The pattern is strong and consistent across four years.
  • The size of the effect: going from a calm month with no cuts to the worst month on record is linked to about a 6 percentage-point difference in retail sales growth.
  • It isn’t just time passing. Even after allowing for the general upward trend in sales, load shedding still explains the drop.
  • It’s an association, not proof of cause. Interest rates, fuel prices and the post-COVID recovery moved over the same years, and the study says so.

03Try it yourself

Architecture

How the parts fit together. Select any box to see what it does in plain words.

05Technical
implementation

How it works

  • Cleaning and alignment: Eskom’s hourly Manual Load Reduction readings were averaged by month. Stats SA’s wide sheet (one column per month) was reshaped into a time series, and year-on-year growth was calculated from the raw rand values at constant 2019 prices.
  • Statistical testing: Pearson correlation with 95% confidence intervals from the Fisher z-transformation: r = −0.649, 95% CI −0.786 to −0.450, n = 49.
  • Delayed effects: tested lags of 0 to 4 months. All were significant (r from −0.649 to −0.698) with no decay, which most likely reflects two eras in the data rather than a precise monthly delay.
  • Regression: OLS of retail growth on load shedding with a linear time trend. Load shedding stays significant (−0.0019 percentage points per MW, p < 0.001) while time on its own does not (p = 0.590).
  • Limits stated up front: correlation is not causation; COVID, interest rates and fuel prices overlap the period; load shedding and time are closely related; and 49 months gives wide intervals, which are reported.
